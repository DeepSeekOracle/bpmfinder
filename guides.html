Start with the job in front of you

Most people arrive here with one of four questions: how do I find the BPM of a song, why does the number look wrong, how do I use BPM in a DJ set, or how do I turn a tempo into a delay time? The guides below are organised around those jobs rather than around keywords.

If you are new, read How to find a song’s BPM first. It gives you the workflow the other guides assume: choose a clean section, read the confidence, check half-time, and confirm with tap tempo. If you already have a number and want to use it, go straight to the DJ, delay, or running guide.

How these guides are made

Each guide is written to answer a real session question, not to fill a page. Where a claim can be measured, we measure it: the accuracy page reports the actual returned tempos, including the failures. Where a number is a convention rather than a rule — genre tempo ranges, for example — we say so.

The free browser BPM tool is the companion to these guides. It analyzes audio locally in your browser; the privacy policy explains what is and is not uploaded.

Have a question the guides do not answer? Contact us and say which page you were reading.

Choose a path

If you are new to tempo: start with How to find a song’s BPM, then read Half-time and double-time. Those two pages cover the mistakes that make every other number confusing.

If you are preparing a DJ set: read BPM for DJs, then Delay times from BPM. The first gives you the planning math; the second gives you the mix math.

If you are producing: read Delay times from BPM and the accuracy test. One shows how tempo becomes a time value; the other shows where the detector can mislead you.

If you are running: go straight to BPM for running and build the arc before you build the playlist.

Every guide links back to the free browser tool, so you can test the idea on a real file instead of reading about it. If a page raises a question that is not answered, the contact page is the fastest way to get it added.
