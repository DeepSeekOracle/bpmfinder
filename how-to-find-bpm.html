How to find a song’s BPM (step-by-step)

A practical method to detect tempo online or in your DAW — without guessing forever.

1. Choose a clear section of the track

Pick 20–40 seconds with a steady pulse. Intros with only pads and no kick often confuse automatic detectors. Verses or drop sections with drums usually work better.

2. Prefer a clean file when you can

Streaming rips with heavy compression still work, but a short WAV export from your DAW is ideal. If the browser fails to decode a format, convert a clip to 16-bit WAV and try again.

3. Run automatic detection

On BPMfinder’s free tool, drop the file and wait for BPM and confidence. High confidence on electronic music is common. Live jazz or free-time sections may score lower — that is normal.

4. Check half and double

If the number feels twice as fast or half as slow as your body wants to move, use half/double. Your counting convention should match the software or dancers you work with.

5. Confirm with tap tempo

Tap along for eight bars. Average the taps. If tap and automatic disagree by more than a few BPM, trust the feel for performance and re-check a cleaner section for production.

6. Write the number where you will see it

Put BPM in the filename, playlist notes, or session template. Tempo only helps if you can find it again under pressure.

Common mistakes

  • Analyzing a 3-second clip with no drums
  • Assuming every track has one “true” BPM with no half-time option
  • Ignoring swing and human timing on live recordings
  • Trusting a single detector without listening
