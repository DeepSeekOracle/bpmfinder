How free browser BPM detection works

Beats per minute (BPM) is how many pulses fit into one minute of music. Automatic detectors look for repeating energy — usually kick drums or strong transients — and estimate how often those events occur. On a clear electronic beat the result is often stable. On ambient music, spoken word, or swing-heavy performances, the detector may lock to a half-time or double-time feel.

Browser tools can decode audio with the Web Audio API and run detection locally. Unreleased demos do not need to be uploaded to a stranger’s server for a basic tempo check. Use half/double buttons, tap tempo, and your ears before you commit a project tempo.

  1. Open the free tool at /app.html.
  2. Drop a 20–40 second section with drums when possible.
  3. Read BPM and confidence.
  4. Try half or double if the groove feels wrong.
  5. Confirm with tap tempo for eight bars.

What the number can and cannot tell you

A BPM value tells you how fast the grid moves. It does not tell you how the track feels, whether the snare is on two and four, or whether the artist counts the groove at half speed. A 70 BPM trap record and a 140 BPM house record can share a pulse; the difference is where the backbeat lands and how the subdivisions are played.

That is why the tool returns a confidence score as well as a number. A high score means the detector found a consistent pulse, not that it read your mind. Treat the score as a reason to double-check, not as a grade. Our measured accuracy test shows exactly where the detector is strong and where it chooses a different counting level.

When half-time and double-time matter

Hip-hop and trap often sit near 70–90 or 140–180 depending on how you count. Drum-and-bass lives near 170–180 but can feel like half when you only track the kick. Pick one counting style and stick to it. Consistency across tracks is more useful than arguing metric theory. See the BPM for DJs guide for the pitch-fader math, and the half-time guide for the counting tests.

How to check a result in five minutes

A BPM number is only useful if you can defend it. The check is short: pick a clean section, run the detector, compare the count with the snare, and confirm with tap tempo. If the four steps agree, write the number down. If they do not, the disagreement tells you which count to investigate.

  1. Choose 20–40 seconds with a steady kick or snare.
  2. Drop the file into the tool and read BPM plus confidence.
  3. Ask where the snare lands: on the slow count, or against a double-time surface?
  4. Tap eight bars and compare the average with the detector.

Most errors are counting errors, not arithmetic errors. A track that reads 140 when you feel 70 is not necessarily a bad detection; it may be the same pulse counted at a different level. FAQ

Is BPMfinder free?

Yes. Guides and the tool are free.

Do I need an account?

No.

What formats work?

MP3, WAV, and FLAC in modern browsers. If one fails, convert a short clip to WAV.

Why does the BPM look half or double?

The detector may be counting a different level of the same pulse. Check the snare and the main backbeat, then choose the count you need.
